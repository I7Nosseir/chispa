## Problems (the pitch)
1. Four different names: La Margrita, La Margherita, LaMargrita, لامارجريتا. Their main hashtag is misspelled (#lamrgrita).
2. Typos in captions: "Tast", "Offar", "Quttro", "shirmp", "whit".
3. Broken phone number: 010062179050 (12 digits, so it can't be a real number) appears in the 30 Sep / 1 Oct delivery post on TikTok and Facebook.
4. Most captions are hashtags only. No hooks, no on-screen text, no reason to order.
5. Huge gap between the viral peaks and the normal reach (13.7M vs ~7K). They got lucky but have no system to repeat it.
6. Reputation: a 1★ review (9 Aug) complained of a burnt base, poor cleanliness and bathroom water near the cashier. The owner replied defensively and reacted with a laughing emoji. Another 1★ (10 Oct) says portions are small for the price and the shrimp pasta was weak. The positive reviews say "like Italy / Europe", praise the chef, and call the staff respectful.

## Content direction
1. Hamza (pizzaiolo) as the recurring face of the brand.
2. "First time trying real Neapolitan": a weekly customer-reaction series (their proven winner).
3. The wood oven explained: "leopard spotting is how Naples does it" (turns the burnt-base complaint into education).
4. Pyramids and tourists: "Tourists rate Egyptian Neapolitan".
5. Honest price-for-value ("real Neapolitan for ~E£250").
6. Everyday comedy plus behind-the-scenes dough work. Check every food claim with the chef first.
Avoid: making religious-gratitude clips a strategy (luck, not a system).

## Free-month deal (agree in writing before starting)
- Scope: content and quick fixes only (one name, bios, pins, caption templates, review-reply script, 4 shoot days, one a week).
- They provide: food and staff time on shoot days, account access through Buffer (they connect their own accounts; never ask for passwords).
- We get: permission to use the results as a case study.
- Baseline = the numbers above, plus their weekly order count.
- End date and the retainer price are agreed up front.

## Publishing tool decision
- Trial: Buffer Free ($0, 3 channels = IG + FB + TikTok, 10 queued posts per channel). Buffer has an MCP connector (manual setup with an API key, mostly create-only).
- Later (once Rakha is live): Metricool Starter (~$20–25/month for 5 brands). It's in Claude's connector directory and connects in one click.

### If they say "we already have a social media guy"
- Never attack him. "Great, then he's half the work. Whoever made the 13.7M video knows how to film."
- Position as an add-on, not a replacement: "I don't want his place. I bring what's missing around him: the brand name, a plan, hooks, the review replies and the numbers. He keeps filming; I give him a system."
- Use their numbers, not opinions: "Is 7K a video normal for an account with 175K? Who checks the phone number and the name on every post?"
- Offer a measurable test: "Give me 2–3 posts a week for a month next to his. We compare the numbers at the end. You decide with data."
- If they still say no: "No problem. Can I send you a one-page report with the quick fixes for free?" (keeps the door open; follow up in 2 weeks).

### Other likely objections
- "You're young / new": "Judge me on the results and the free month, not my age. Here are the Grapes and Rihla numbers."
- "We're doing fine, we have 175K": "You are. That's exactly why it's worth fixing; you're leaving orders on the table from an audience you already have."
- "How much after the month?": give the range only after they see results (proposed social management packages: 6k / 10–12k / 15–20k EGP per month; founder approves the price first).
- "Send me something on WhatsApp": "Sure, and can I visit for 15 minutes this week to show you on your phone?" Always try for the meeting.
