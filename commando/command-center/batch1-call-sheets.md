# Batch 1 call sheets: Tier A, 27 businesses (17 Oct 2026)

Batch 1 = every Tier A lead in the Command Center: 20 in Egypt (call) and 7 in Dubai (message first). Each one is rated for realism: **Go now** (owner-run, winnable), **Pilot only** (one small fixed job) or **Park for later** (too big or already served; come back with case studies). Competitor facts come from the Google Maps pulls of 11 Oct 2026. **Check each fact on your phone before you call; reviews change.**

## The playbook: what's proven, and how each call uses it

Belfort's competitor-insight opener is one tactic. Each call sheet below combines it with the other methods that hold up, listed here with how strong the evidence is.

| Tactic | Where it comes from | How strong | How you use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **State the reason for the call early** | Gong (secondary summaries report about 2.1× success; I couldn't find the figure on Gong's own pages) | Medium | "The reason I'm calling is…" inside the first 10 seconds |
| **Permission-based opener** | Gong, 300M calls: 11.18% success vs a 1.5% baseline | Strong (large dataset, vendor-run) | "Can I take 30 seconds to explain why I called?" |
| **Don't open with "Did I catch you at a bad time?"** | Gong: about 2% success, the weakest opener tested | Strong | Never use it, even though it sounds polite |
| **Competitor or market insight first** | Belfort (Straight Line); also the "Challenger" approach | Expert method | One true, public fact about a nearby competitor |
| **Poke-the-bear question** | Josh Braun | Expert method | A neutral question that lets them discover the gap themselves, instead of you pitching it |
| **Problem before solution, no ROI promises** | Gong: ROI language lowered cold-email success by about 15% | Medium (email data) | Talk about their missing link, not "I'll grow your revenue X%" |
| **Three Tens (certainty in idea, you, plan)** | Belfort | Expert method | The fact (idea) → one real result (you) → a small free first step (plan) |
| **No-oriented ask** | Chris Voss, *Never Split the Difference* | Expert method | "Would it be a bad idea if I sent you the audit?" Saying no feels safe, and here no means yes |
| **Interest ask in messages** | Gong: an interest CTA got about 30% vs about 15% for a specific meeting ask (cold email) | Medium | End WhatsApp and DMs with "Open to seeing it?", not "Can we meet Tuesday at 3?" |
| **Give first (reciprocity)** | Cialdini, *Influence* | Strong (decades of research) | The free audit, or 3 free images |
| **Looping, at most twice** | Belfort | Expert method (shortened) | Acknowledge → one new fact → ask the small thing again |
| **Future pacing** | Belfort / NLP selling | Expert method | One sentence picturing their customer once it's fixed |
| **Warm, calm tone** | Voss; Gong | Strong | A friendly voice beats a perfect script; speak your own Egyptian Arabic |

**Why would they talk to a 19-year-old freelancer?**

Most of them won't, if you sell "marketing management". That's what agencies with teams sell. Some will, if you bring:
1. **A specific broken thing** they didn't know about (the Google Form, the Yellow Pages links, the wrong Instagram). Nobody cares about your age when you point at their own listing.
2. **A small fixed job with a price**, not a monthly retainer: the listing fixed, a one-page site, review replies for a month, 3 product images.
3. **Proof:** Grapes Holiday and Rihla are real numbers from real money.
4. **Speed and direct access:** you answer WhatsApp yourself, the same day.

**Who to aim at now:**
- Owner-run, single-location businesses where the owner is in the building: clinics, salons, small restaurants, padel clubs.
- Not groups with branches, and not places whose social media already works.
- After 2–3 local case studies with before/after numbers, go back to the parked ones.

**Your age and size:**
- Don't lead with your age, and don't hide it if asked. "I'm 19, and here's what I did for Grapes" is fine.
- Don't say "we" as if you had a team you don't have.

**Getting past the order line, the right way.** I won't dig up managers' personal mobile numbers. Calling a private number nobody gave you is exactly what makes you a cold caller, and it can get you blocked or reported. Use the routes the business itself publishes:
1. **Ask for a name, not a transfer.** "Who's the manager, and when is he usually in?" Then call back and ask for him by name. A name changes how the next person treats you.
2. **The Instagram or Facebook DM.** In restaurants and salons, the owner or the marketing person reads it, not the order-taker.
3. **The business email or "for business" number** in their Instagram bio, Facebook "About" page or website. They published it for exactly this.
4. **LinkedIn.** Search "[business] owner" or "marketing manager" and send a short connection note with the one fact.
5. **Walk in** at a quiet hour (3–5 PM for restaurants, late morning for salons and gyms) and ask for the manager. This works best for places near you: Zayed, October, Hadayek, Haram.
6. **Mutual contacts.** Ask friends and GUC classmates whether anyone knows the owner.

**The call in order (each sheet follows it):**
1. **Permission + reason** (first 4–10 seconds): who you are, why you're calling, 30 seconds?
2. **Insight:** the competitor fact, short.
3. **Poke:** one neutral question about the gap. Then stop talking.
4. **Qualify:** 2–3 questions, and listen.
5. **Certainty:** one real result.
6. **No-oriented small ask:** the free audit.
7. **Loop** once or twice on an objection, then let it go.
8. **Future pace,** then get the name, the WhatsApp and the follow-up day.

**What I left out of Belfort, on purpose:**
- Pressure, looping 3–5 times, fake urgency, closing on the first call, and "say whatever closes".
- He was convicted of fraud, so keep his structure and leave his ethics.
- A second "no" ends the call politely. Note a follow-up for 3 months later.

**Rules:**
- Use only true, public facts (Google Maps, their own posts), and check them on your phone before each call.
- Never insult the competitor, and never say a competitor is your client.
- If they ask how you know: "It's public on Google Maps. I compared the listings in your area."
- **Dubai:** message first. Call only after they reply.
