## 1. How colour works — the three dials

Every colour is three decisions. Change any one and the meaning changes.

| Dial | OKLCH name | What it does to meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Hue** | H (0–360°) | the "family" — red, blue, green… gives the basic association |
| **Chroma / saturation** | C (0–0.37) | high = energy, youth, excitement, cheapness if overdone; low = calm, sophistication, sincerity, luxury |
| **Lightness / value** | L (0–1) | dark = authority, depth, luxury, night; light = openness, softness, youth, day |

Use **OKLCH** for all reasoning and scales: it is perceptually uniform, so equal numeric steps look like equal visual steps (unlike HSL, where "50% lightness" yellow and blue look wildly different). The script converts every hex to OKLCH.

**Warm vs cool:** warm (red→yellow, ~20°–100°) advances, feels close, appetising, human; cool (green→violet, ~140°–300°) recedes, feels calm, distant, technical. Warm neutrals (cream, cognac, taupe) are the backbone of most "quiet luxury".

**Value contrast beats hue contrast.** People read structure by light/dark differences. Test any palette in greyscale: if the hierarchy disappears, the palette is weak.

## 2. Evidence — what research actually supports

- **Labrecque & Milne (2012), JAMS:** blue → competence and trust; red → excitement; pink → sincerity; purple/black → sophistication. **Saturation**: high saturation → excitement, competence, ruggedness; low saturation → sincerity and sophistication. **Value**: darker → sophistication, ruggedness; lighter → sincerity, excitement.
- **Differentiation:** brands that use a colour different from category norms are easier to recognise and recall, as long as it still fits the category (Labrecque & Milne 2013 follow-up on colour differentiation).
- **Context dominates:** the same hue reads differently by culture, product category, neighbouring colours and amount. Treat "colour meanings" as *tendencies*, not laws.
- **Colour blindness:** ~8% of men (deuteranomaly most common, ~5–6%) and ~0.5% of women have a colour-vision deficiency. Red/green pairs collapse to brown-yellow for them.

## 3. Hue families — meanings and hex anchors

Each row: the hue's core reading, how saturation/value shift it, and named anchors (approximate values used by luxury and heritage brands; verify any brand claim before quoting it).

### Red (≈ 20–30° OKLCH)
- **Core:** energy, passion, urgency, appetite, danger, love. In luxury: only deep and desaturated.
- **Bright** (#E10600, #D7263D): sale, sport, warning — loud, cheap if large.
- **Deep** — burgundy, oxblood, claret, wine (#6E1423 "cellar burgundy", #5A1A22 oxblood, #7A1F2B, #4A0E17): craftsmanship, wine, leather, aged quality, ceremony, sensuality. Ages well. Use as accent or anchor.
- **Watch:** large saturated red + black = horror/devil/aggression. Red with green = Christmas.

### Orange (≈ 40–60°)
- **Core:** friendly, energetic, affordable, youthful, creative.
- **Burnt / rust / cognac / copper** (#9A4A1F rust, #8B4A2B cognac, #B87333 copper): warmth, leather, whisky, craft — the luxury way to use orange.
- **Bright orange** (#F97316): fun, budget, approachable (Ignite Uni uses it — avoid for sister brands).

### Yellow & gold (≈ 70–95°)
- **Core yellow:** optimism, attention, youth, caution. Hard to read on white.
- **Gold** (#C69B3C 24k-style, #B8935A antique, #A8834A brass, #D4AF37 metallic gold, #E5C07B champagne highlight): status, achievement, permanence, reward. Reads as gold only on a deep base or as a thin line/foil. On white, gold becomes mustard — use a darker brass (#8C6A2F) there.
- **Champagne / sand** (#E8D9B5, #BFB290 — Nova's sand): soft luxury, hospitality, quiet calm.

### Green (≈ 140–165°)
- **Core:** growth, health, money, nature, safety, "go".
- **Emerald / bottle / racing** (#0D4C3C, #00573F, #004225 British racing green): old money, heritage, rarity, gemstones, gentlemen's clubs. Pairs with gold and cream.
- **Sage / olive** (#8A9A5B, #6B705C): calm, organic, wellness.
- **Neon/lime:** tech, gaming, youth.
- In Egypt/MENA: strongly linked to Islam, nature and prosperity — positive; avoid flippant uses.

### Teal / cyan (≈ 190–220°)
- **Core:** clarity, freshness, modern health, tech, calm intelligence.
- **Deep teal / petrol** (#0F4C5C, #134E4A): sophisticated, intellectual, modern-heritage. Good bridge between blue trust and green growth.

### Blue (≈ 240–265°)
- **Core:** trust, competence, stability, intelligence, calm, corporate. The most-liked colour globally and the most-used in finance, tech and education → hard to stand out with mid-blues.
- **Navy / midnight / ink** (#0F172A midnight, #1F3A5F navy, #14213D, #0B1F3A): authority, heritage, old-money confidence, formal tailoring.
- **Bright blue** (#0099FF): tech, friendly, digital — Sparq Study's signature (avoid for sister brands).

### Purple / violet (≈ 290–320°)
- **Core:** royalty, mystery, creativity, spirituality, premium (historically expensive dye).
- **Deep aubergine / plum** (#3D1F3D, #4B2142): luxury, night, perfume, couture. Easily reads "beauty/feminine" in some markets.

### Pink (≈ 350–10°)
- **Core:** sincerity, care, romance, playfulness. Desaturated rose/dusty pink (#D8A7A7, #C9A9A6) = soft luxury, beauty; hot pink = loud, Gen-Z.

### Brown (≈ 40–60°, low L)
- **Core:** earth, reliability, warmth, craft, coffee, leather, wood.
- **Espresso / cocoa / chocolate** (#3B2A24, #52423B Nova cocoa, #4A3428): grounded premium, artisan, cosy. With cream = "café/atelier"; with gold = "heritage leather".

## 5. Harmony rules

Measure angles on the OKLCH hue wheel. Harmony makes a palette *feel* intentional; proportion makes it *look* expensive.

| Scheme | Construction | Feels | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Monochrome** | one hue, several L/C steps | calm, minimal, premium, confident | luxury, personal brands, fashion |
| **Analogous** | 2–3 hues within ±30–45° | harmonious, warm or cool family | quiet luxury (cognac + burgundy + espresso) |
| **Complementary** | ~180° apart | high tension, energy | accents only; sport, youth |
| **Split-complementary** | base + two hues at 180°±30° | lively but balanced | education/youth brands that still want order |
| **Triadic** | 3 hues 120° apart | playful, bold | kids, gaming, festivals |
| **Tetradic / square** | 4 hues | busy; needs strong dominance | rarely for brands |
| **Neutral + one accent** | neutrals 85–95% + one hue | modern, editorial | editorial, consulting, premium education |
| **Dark + metal** | deep base + gold/brass | black-tie luxury | hospitality, jewellery, premium personal brands |

## 6. Proportion and hierarchy

- **60-30-10:** 60% base, 30% anchor/secondary, 10% accent. Luxury often shifts to **70-25-5** — even less accent.
- **One accent rule:** a brand has one signature colour people remember. Two accents compete.
- **Greyscale test:** convert to greyscale; title, body, background must still separate.
- **Contrast targets (WCAG 2.x):** body text ≥ 4.5:1 (AA), ≥ 7:1 (AAA, premium target); large text ≥ 3:1; UI icons/borders ≥ 3:1.
- **Tonal scale:** generate 9 steps per colour (L ≈ 0.97 → 0.20) for UI states, charts, gradients; keep chroma slightly lower at the extremes to avoid neon tints and muddy shades.

## 7. Luxury formulas

What makes a palette read as premium (cross-checked across luxury palette guides):
1. **Few hues** (2–3 + neutrals).
2. **Deep values** for at least one anchor (L < 0.30).
3. **Low–mid chroma** (C < 0.12 for most colours; accent may reach ~0.15).
4. **One controlled accent**, used ≤ 10%.
5. **Warm neutrals instead of pure white/black.**
6. **A metal cue** (gold/brass line, foil monogram) — small.
7. **Texture and light do half the work**: paper grain, linen, leather, soft directional light, deep shadows.
8. **Consistency**: same palette in photos (grade), wardrobe, set, print.

Cheap signals to avoid: rainbow palettes, neon gradients, pure #FF0000/#00FF00, gold on white at small size, more than one saturated colour, drop-shadowed gold text.

## 9. Egypt / MENA cultural notes

- **Green:** strongly positive — Islam, nature, prosperity; use respectfully, avoid jokes or "danger" contexts.
- **White:** purity, peace, Hajj, weddings; also mourning in some contexts — fine as background.
- **Black:** elegance and formality; also mourning. Balanced by warm accents it reads luxury, not grief.
- **Gold:** wealth, celebration, weddings — very positive; overuse = gaudy (avoid "too much gold" which reads Gulf-bling or wedding-hall).
- **Red:** love, danger, power; deep reds read refined, bright red reads sale/sport.
- **Blue:** protection (evil-eye blue #1E5AA8 / turquoise), trust; very common in Egyptian education → hard to stand out.
- **Ancient Egypt:** black = fertility/rebirth, white = purity, red = life/chaos, green = renewal, blue = heavens, gold = divinity/eternity — usable as subtle storytelling, never as costume.
- Students in Egypt (Gen-Z) respond to modern, minimal, "global" aesthetics; parents read deep navy/green/burgundy + gold as "serious, expensive, trustworthy".

## 10. Screen, print and video

- **Screen first** (social): design in sRGB hex; check on a phone at low brightness.
- **Print:** CMYK values from the script are approximations; ask the printer for a proof; gold should become foil or a Pantone metallic (e.g., Pantone 871 C) on premium pieces.
- **Dark mode / dark slides:** swap base and anchor; keep accent; re-check contrast.
- **Video grade:** pick a white balance and contrast that echo the palette (warm 3200–4000K for whisky/cognac moods; neutral 5000–5600K for editorial). Wardrobe and set props should carry the palette so the logo isn't needed.

## 11. Decoding a reference — worked example: *Lucifer* (TV, 2016–2021)

Observed (costume and set sources): black tailored suits, dark blue or black shirts, red pocket square, onyx ring; a burgundy tuxedo jacket for a ceremony; the Lux piano bar — dark wood, black lacquer, warm amber/whisky light, gold fittings; charm, wit, control.

| Element | Feeling | Keep? |
|---|---|---|
| Black suit | control, power, elegance | ✅ anchor (warm off-black, not #000) |
| Amber/whisky light | warmth, charm, indulgence | ✅ as cognac accent or photo grade |
| Burgundy tux | ceremony, refinement | ✅ deep accent (≤10%) |
| Gold fittings | status, reward | ✅ thin metal cue |
| Red pocket square / fire | devil, danger | ❌ drop saturated red and flame imagery |
| Night club neon | nightlife | ❌ wrong for education |

Reference DNA → "Black Tie" / "Whisky Lounge" / "Velvet Night" families from §8, tuned for the brand.
